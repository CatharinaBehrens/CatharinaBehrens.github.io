### _In Progress_

### From Equal Access to Unequal Returns: Networks and Gender

With Michelle Hansch, Jan Nimczik, and Alexandra Spitz-Oener.

<details>
<summary>Abstract</summary>

<p>Does the effect of coworker networks on career trajectories differ between men and women? We examine this question using linked administrative data on workers in East Germany before and after German reunification, which provides a unique natural experiment. Under the GDR's centrally planned economy, we show that the state-governed allocation of workers to jobs resulted in more gender balance across occupations, and more equal access to high-quality networks for men and women. This setting allows us to study gender differences in the returns to professional networks formed before reunification as workers subsequently built their careers in a market economy. We find striking gender differences in these returns. For men, having a highly educated coworker is associated with a greater likelihood of achieving top labor market outcomes. For women, however, the estimated returns are weaker for top labor market outcomes, with wage gains concentrated at the lower end of the distribution. Our findings suggest that gender differences in career outcomes cannot be explained by unequal access to high-quality professional networks alone. Even when men and women have comparable networks, highly educated coworkers do not necessarily translate into comparable career opportunities.</p>

</details>
